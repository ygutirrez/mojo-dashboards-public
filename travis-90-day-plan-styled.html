🦟 90-Day Operations Manager Development Plan

Travis Johnson · Mosquito Joe of Miami · February 2026

Current State

  • Title: Operations Manager
  • Comp: Salary + 0.5% quarterly profit bonus
  • Current responsibilities: Fleet, equipment, inventory/chemical ordering, tech hiring (shared w/ Bev), tech training
  • Strengths: Reliable, techs respect him, hands-on knowledge of every route and service
  • Growth areas: Formal management skills, KPI ownership, delegation, decision-making autonomy

Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1–30)

Theme: "Own the Numbers"

Week 1–2: Dashboard & KPI Ownership

  • Walk Travis through every dashboard (tech, ops, owners)
  • Give him daily access — bookmark on his phone/tablet
  • Assign him ownership of these weekly KPIs:
    • Jobs/tech/day (target: 10+)
    • Revenue/tech/day (target: $950+)
    • Callback rate (target: <1%)
    • On-time arrival %
    • Chemical usage per route
  • Set up a Friday afternoon "numbers review" (15 min, just him + you)

Week 3–4: Process Documentation

  • Have Travis document his current fleet/equipment processes (even rough notes)
  • Document the chemical ordering workflow
  • Document the tech onboarding checklist (what he and Bev currently do)
  • Why: Forces him to think systematically, creates backup if he's ever out

Monthly Check-in (Day 30)

Review: Does he understand the KPIs? Is he checking them without being asked?
Feedback: What's going well, what feels overwhelming
Adjust: Add or remove responsibilities as needed

Phase 2: Ownership (Days 31–60)

Theme: "Make the Call"

Week 5–6: Decision Authority

  • Define clear decision-making boundaries:
    • Travis decides alone: Tech scheduling changes, supply orders <$500, equipment maintenance, route adjustments, customer callbacks
    • Travis recommends, you approve: Tech hiring/firing, equipment purchases >$500, policy changes, customer refunds >$200
    • Owners decide: Salary changes, new service offerings, major vendor changes
  • Start letting him handle customer escalations (Bev transitions these to him)
  • Let him run the weekly tech meeting solo

Week 7–8: Training & Development

  • Enroll in ONE training resource (pick best fit):
    • NPMA QualityPro Manager Training (if QualityPro accredited)
    • Dale Carnegie Leadership Training (Miami sessions)
    • Coursera: "Operations Management" by Wharton (self-paced)
  • Connect with SCORE mentor (free, schedule monthly)
  • Book: "Turn the Ship Around" by L. David Marquet

Monthly Check-in (Day 60)

Review: Is he making decisions independently? How do the techs respond?
Feedback: Review 2–3 decisions he made — what went well, what he'd do differently
Metric: Are KPIs improving, stable, or declining under his watch?

Phase 3: Leadership (Days 61–90)

Theme: "Develop Others"

Week 9–10: Build His Team

  • Identify a lead tech (or 2) who can be Travis's eyes on the road
  • Have Travis start doing monthly 1-on-1s with each tech (even 10 min)
  • Transition tech hiring FULLY to Travis (Bev supports admin/paperwork only)
  • Have him propose one operational improvement — let him own it end to end

Week 11–12: Strategic Thinking

  • Include Travis in one business planning conversation
  • Ask him to present the ops KPIs at the next partner meeting
  • Have him identify the #1 thing costing the business money that he could fix
  • Discuss: what would he need to manage a second location?

90-Day Review

Evaluate:
• Can he run daily operations without your involvement?
• Are techs performing at or above prior levels?
• Has he made at least 3 independent decisions that worked?
• Does he think about the business or just the tasks?

Next steps: Compensation review tied to KPIs he now owns. Set next 90-day goals.

Quick Wins (Start This Week)

  1. Send him the tech dashboard link — "this is your scoreboard now"
  2. Next ops call that comes to you, redirect to Travis
  3. At the next team meeting, publicly reinforce his authority
  4. Stop approving his chemical orders — just let him run it

Success Metrics at 90 Days

  • Travis checks dashboards daily without being asked
  • Weekly ops meeting run by Travis (you attend occasionally, not always)
  • Customer escalation resolution without owner involvement: >80%
  • Tech productivity maintained or improved
  • At least one process improvement Travis identified and implemented
  • Yoel spends 5+ fewer hours/week on operations

The goal isn't to make Travis perfect in 90 days. It's to shift him from "doing ops tasks" to "managing operations." The tasks stay the same — the mindset changes.
